# Newsletter Specialist v1 — pilot specification

**Entrypoint:** manual portable skill at `skills/newsletter-editorial/SKILL.md`; current implementation status is in the [architecture](../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).  
**First test:** Improve an actual, source-backed issue of the business's own publication; do not generate bulk newsletters from generic prompts.

**Shared foundation:** [source-grounded cross-channel research](../../core/RESEARCH-AND-EVIDENCE-CONTRACT.md) and [quality/release gates](../../core/QUALITY-GATE-CONTRACT.md). This existing newsletter pilot's research and craft skills are specialized applications of those general contracts, not a second independent source of company truth.

## Mission
Create an editorial issue that a clearly defined reader would want to open and finish, containing real specificity, original argument or narrative value, and only defensible facts.

## Professional work
1. Establish publication, audience, reader promise and business objective; distinguish customer-facing education from an audience of builders. **Before proposing topics, complete the source-linked research pack in `skills/copywriting/RESEARCH-WORKFLOW.md`**: product, audience/VoC, brand, comparable content and research synthesis.
2. Inspect authentic source material (approved journal facts, working-session excerpts, decisions, actual test/artifacts) and separate observations from interpretation.
3. Offer a small number of genuinely different editorial angles; propose skipping a weak story rather than inventing drama.
4. Write an editorial brief with the core point, specific evidence, what reader gains and what cannot be claimed.
5. Research any outside context needed and note source dates; never treat prior generated marketing drafts as primary factual evidence.
6. Draft with flexible structure and strong editorial judgment; this is a craft requirement for the *agent*, not a mandate for the *author to sound like an authority*. Avoid uniform outlines and generic uplifting endings. No invented scenes, anecdotes, metrics or quotes.
7. Independently check claims, private/public status, voice, relevance, narrative integrity and reader usefulness; revise within the run's revision budget (`max_revision_rounds`, set by the pack and workspace; see the [specialist contract](../../core/SPECIALIST-CONTRACT.md)), then ask for new instructions.
8. Return the draft, source ledger, critical editorial notes, alternatives and what owner feedback is needed.

## Boundary with a change-log tool
A change-log tool (such as a daily journal or build log) captures the factual change record. A **Session Story Miner** (future authorized tool) preserves the owner's questions, doubts, false starts, conflicts and conclusions. The newsletter specialist can use both, but neither source grants permission to expose private data.

## Core skills (focused pilot versions available)
- Audience and publication research.
- Copywriting/editorial method informed by multiple sources, with course study as one input.
- Story selection and source verification.
- Editing and voice authenticity.
- Reader relationship and newsletter-native structure.
