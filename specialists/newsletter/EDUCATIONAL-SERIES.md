# Educational newsletter series — reusable planning method

**Purpose:** Help the Studio Director and newsletter specialist run a continuing **guide** based on authentic project experience, instead of producing a list of weekly changelogs or prematurely turning a newsletter into a book.

## Distinguish the learning path from source chronology

A company may begin with an old repo, a partial workflow, or an existing prototype. A reader starting a new project needs a **learning sequence** (problem, research, requirements, design, build, verify) rather than a strict chronological list of historical incidents. Preserve actual dates and order inside each story, but teach fundamentals first.

**Inputs:** business editorial promise and audience, existing original project artifacts, prior issues, factual/privacy evidence, current implementation status, constraints and expert references.

**Director's decisions**
1. Define what a reader should understand/do by the end of the series, then divide it into a small set of real stage questions. Do not prescribe a fixed number of episodes before source research.
2. Map each question to primary artifacts and gaps. A future feature discussion must be labeled proposed/in-progress/verified-built/released.
3. Separate (a) general explanatory guidance and attributed external research from (b) this project's documented choices and limitations.
4. Prioritize issues with a genuine reader problem and demonstrable example. If one step needs multiple episodes to be useful, allow it; if two steps fit in one, combine them.
5. For document/tutorial issues, answer **what document, why it exists, how to write a minimum version, what the original project wrote, what changed during review, and when it may be unnecessary**. These are educational requirements, not mandatory heading labels.
6. Keep a lightweight editable worksheet/template where it adds value, with a filled *source-backed* example. No copyrighted third-party worksheet copying or fabricated metrics.
7. For sequential issue design, include a natural connection to the next reader question without a compulsory motivational or sales CTA.
8. Keep any later book, course or multi-channel adaptation separate. **Newsletter quality is the current deliverable**, not book production.

## Proving the craft

- Research → material selection → angle → proof map → substantial draft → independent source audit → /unslop editorial diagnosis → human/owner CUB → versioned adjustment.
- Compare not only the author’s enjoyment but the reader’s ability to follow one real case and adapt an exercise.
- Don't turn an earlier user rejection into a global ban. A reader guide that needs longer technical explanation should keep it despite style flags.
- The specialist may refuse to publish a thin issue or request a missing first-hand statement.
- After an owner approves a private draft, save the source and QA bundle; separate publish authorization is required later.

## Portable interface to product-building series

- General learning phases: discover problem, research intended users, define scope, plan requirements, prototype, choose design, build/test components, release and learn.
- The business workspace declares what phases **actually occurred**. Never project a standard startup journey onto a company where the records show otherwise.
- A case may have an undocumented gap; use that as an honest uncertainty or useful research question rather than creating an imaginary founder interview.
- Expert sources (e.g. YC user discovery, concise PRDs) can inform questions, but should be attributed and not passed off as the business’s own practice.
