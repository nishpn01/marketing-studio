# Comparative editorial research — methods, not voices

**Status:** initial examples inspected, November 2026. Benchmark references are sources of craft ideas, not permission to reproduce style, biography or proprietary content.

## Selected models

### Marketer Milk — authentic practitioner narrative
- [I Made a Decision](https://www.newsletter.marketermilk.com/p/i-made-a-decision), May 5, 2026
- [What I've Learned as a Head of Growth](https://www.newsletter.marketermilk.com/p/what-i-ve-learned-as-a-head-of-growth), Sep 22, 2026
**Observed strengths:** honest non-linear career/build decisions, concrete details, admissions of unsuccessful experiments, personal voice and a clear reason for an issue to exist. Dense link curation is a separate component and is not mandatory.
**Adaptation:** let the author's actual question, changed mind and evidence drive structure. Never borrow the writer's level of authority, personal feelings, anecdotes or numbers. Do not imitate slang.

### Every — evidence-rich product/agent case study
- [Why We Replaced Personal Agents With One for the Whole Company](https://every.to/source-code/why-we-replaced-personal-agents-with-one-for-the-whole-company), Oct 20, 2026
- [Why We Handed Our Agent’s Infrastructure to Anthropic](https://every.to/source-code/why-we-handed-our-agent-s-infrastructure-to-anthropic), Oct 8, 2026
**Observed strengths:** exact decision, clearly specified problem, real technical and organizational tradeoffs, attributed sources, explanation of what changed and what was discovered. Details are given enough room.
**Adaptation:** for builder issues, show real documents/decisions and how the system behaved. Don't copy another team's dramatic anecdote or overcomplicate with jargon.

### Lenny's Newsletter — structured reader-focused research
- [How to Make People Care About Your Startup](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-make-people-care-about-your), Kristen Lowe, Aug 11, 2026 (partly paid)
**Observed strengths:** defines a real audience problem, supports an organized explanatory model, distinguishes different cases and gives the reader choices. Limits: framework-heavy and authored from professional experience we may not share.
**Adaptation:** use research depth and clear distinction of cases, not an expert persona or unsupported founder archetype. Paywall limits review, so do not claim to have studied inaccessible sections.

### Ask a Manager — practical and calibrated career advice
- [Should I Stop Using AI to Write My Cover Letters?](https://www.askamanager.org/category/ai-at-work), Sep 28, 2026 (find linked article in archive)
- [This Is How to Write a Good Cover Letter](https://www.askamanager.org/2024/11/this-is-how-to-write-a-good-cover-letter.html), Nov 12, 2024
**Observed strengths:** starts from actual reader uncertainty, uses specific examples, explains exceptions and gives candid advice without a motivational template. Author has professional hiring experience.
**Adaptation:** Job Search with AI can demonstrate tested tools, checklists and claim checks but must NOT pretend the business founder is an experienced hiring manager or promise interviews/offers.

## General editorial decisions

1. **Two streams, two reader contracts.** Job-search writing solves actual reader questions and includes practical examples; building writing goes into real decisions, tradeoffs and artifact evidence.
2. **Tone:** informed, curious, understated, specific. A founder documenting their learning is credible without pretending to be a guru. Avoid universal commandments, absolute guarantees, artificial confidence and borrowed expertise.
3. **Substance beats issue quotas:** use a significant reader problem, relevant research and owned/source-linked evidence. Reject a weekly change log unless it yields an actual story.
4. **Length follows insight and evidence:** no automatic 400–600-word cap; no arbitrary 1,500-word minimum either.
5. **No uniform structure or required sales CTA:** a detailed how-to, narrative story, annotated artifact, comparison or carefully sourced question can each be a newsletter.
6. **Copywriting course principles are instruments, not identity:** use research, RIOA and CUB to make an issue relevant and comprehensible; don't convert an educational newsletter into a direct-response sales pitch.
7. **Never invent a founder anecdote or private motive.** An unapproved activity journal is insufficient evidence for exact first-person feelings or quotes.

## Questions to test with real owner/reader feedback

- Which examples felt readable all the way through and why?
- Does an issue reveal reasoning or merely report actions?
- Can a reader do/understand something new, and is that benefit actually demonstrated?
- Are any confident sentences larger than the evidence behind them?
- Is it worth an email, or should it be a short social update / note / no post?

This is a working editorial research note, not a proof that the new specialist has achieved target quality.
