# Copywriting: reader and message evaluation

**Purpose:** Shared craft-review method for editorial and conversion copy; not a replacement for factual source verification.

1. Can the specified reader, at their likely awareness level, understand the piece without unsupported audience assumptions?
2. Does the central reader promise actually get fulfilled? Is the useful idea or genuine offer clear?
3. Apply the five reader questions: relevance, novelty, payoff, mechanism and credibility. Cite exact excerpts where the answer is weak.
4. **CUB:** Diagnose potentially *confusing, unbelievable or boring* passages. An AI's critique is a **simulation**, not tested reader feedback; never claim real CUB without real readers.
5. **So what / prove it:** Identify passages with no reader purpose, unexplained leaps, weak objections or proof gaps. Send factual assertions to independent fact QA rather than certifying them here.
6. Consider hierarchy, transitions, excessive jargon, empty slogans, repetitive prose and channel-appropriate length.
7. Judge **channel fit:** A newsletter need not sell; a landing page should clearly explain a supported offer and action. Avoid invented outcomes, credibility, urgency or scarcity.

Return actionable findings with an exact excerpt, reader problem, suggested change and `must` or `should` severity. Reserve `must` for material failures of integrity, reader promise or basic comprehension. Mechanical lint and model review do not prove human usefulness.

The fact reviewer evaluates source truth independently. Neither reviewer can approve, publish, spend, or change the owner's direction.
