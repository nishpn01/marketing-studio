# Copywriting: message construction and proof

**Purpose:** Shared writing method that adapts to a newsletter, tutorial, landing page, sales letter, or another channel. Do not turn all formats into sales copy.

- Identify **one primary reader**, **one central idea**, and the actual reader payoff. A commercial page also needs a truthful offer and intended action; an educational piece may not need a conversion CTA.
- Plan the **order of proof**, not a compulsory outline. What does a skeptical reader need to know first? Which real artifact, explanation or source makes the next conclusion credible?
- Open with relevant specificity: a supported observation, useful question or real tension. Never invent urgency, reader experiences, founder emotion or dramatic scenes.
- Translate supported features into concrete consequences *without inventing observed outcomes*. Explain the mechanism: how it works, what it changes in principle, and what remains untested.
- Address relevant objections with evidence. Objection → claim → proof → benefit can guide reasoning but must not become a rigid visible formula.
- Give the substance and its tradeoffs appropriate space. Avoid uniform section lengths, generic motivational endings, borrowed expert personas and a list of benefits with no proof.
- Close for the channel: earned insight or actionable check for educational work; supported offer and clear CTA for conversion work. No fabricated guarantees, testimonials, scarcity or hype.

**Five reader questions:** Why is this relevant? What's new or specific? What do I gain? How does the mechanism work? Why trust these claims?

**Genre boundaries:** Newsletters prioritize original insight, truthful personal perspective, clarity and ongoing reader relationship. Conversion pages prioritize offer clarity, buyer awareness, differentiating proof, objections and next action. SEO tutorials prioritize searcher intent, genuinely complete explanations and original value, not keyword stuffing.

**Revision:** For each significant passage ask **so what?** (reader value) and **prove it?** (source and argument). Preserve strong evidence and author voice. Recheck changed factual claims. Original source/visibility rules outrank persuasive tactics.
